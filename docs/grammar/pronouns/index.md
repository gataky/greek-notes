# Greek Pronouns

Pronouns stand in for a noun: *I, this, who, someone*. Greek has the same kinds as English, but most of them change form.

| Type | Greek | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Personal](personal.md) | εγώ, εσύ, με, σε, του | I, you, me, to him |
| [Possessive](possessive.md) | μου, σου, δικός μου | my, your, mine |
| [Demonstrative](demonstrative.md) | αυτός, εκείνος, τέτοιος, τόσος | this, that, such, so much |
| [Interrogative](interrogative.md) | ποιος, τι, πόσος | who, what, how much |
| [Relative](relative.md) | που, ο οποίος, όποιος, ό,τι | who, which, whoever, whatever |
| [Indefinite](indefinite.md) | κάποιος, κάτι, κανένας, τίποτα | someone, something, no one, nothing |
| [Reflexive and emphatic](reflexive.md) | ο εαυτός μου, ο ίδιος, μόνος μου | myself, the same, by myself |

Three things hold across all of them:

- **Most decline like adjectives**, agreeing in gender, number and case: ποι**ος** άντρας, ποι**α** γυναίκα, ποι**ο** παιδί. See [adjectives](../adjectives.md).
- **Most work alone or with a noun**: **αυτό** είναι καλό (this is good), **αυτό** το κρασί (this wine).
- **Masculine covers mixed groups**: **αυτοί** είναι οι γονείς μου (these are my parents).
