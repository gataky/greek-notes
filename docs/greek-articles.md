# Greek Articles

Greek articles change with **gender** (masculine, feminine, neuter), **case** (nominative, accusative, genitive) and **number** (singular, plural). The article usually tells you a noun's gender and case faster than the noun's ending does.

## Indefinite article (a, an)

In everyday Greek, **έναν** is common before any word, while **μιαν** is rare.

### No plural: just drop the article

Where English uses "some" or nothing at all, Greek usually has no article.

- Έχω **φίλους** στην Αθήνα. (I have friends in Athens.)
- Αγοράζω **μήλα**. (I'm buying apples.)

## Patterns that help

- **Neuter nominative = accusative**, always: το/το, τα/τα, ένα/ένα.
- **Genitive plural is των** for every gender.
- **Masculine and neuter share the genitive singular:** του (definite) and ενός (indefinite).
- **οι** is the nominative plural for both masculine and feminine.

## How each case is used

### Nominative: the subject

Use the nominative for:

- **The subject**, the person or thing doing the action.
- **The word after "to be"** (είμαι, γίνομαι) that renames or describes the subject.

| English | Greek |
| --- | --- |
| The baker bakes the bread. | **Ο** φούρναρης ψήνει το ψωμί. |
| The child is sleeping. | **Το** παιδί κοιμάται. |
| Maria is a teacher. | **Η** Μαρία είναι δασκάλα. |
| A friend is coming tonight. | **Ένας** φίλος έρχεται απόψε. |

### Accusative: the object, prepositions, time and distance

Use the accusative for:

- **The direct object**, the person or thing the action is done to.
- **After most prepositions**, especially σε, για, με, από.
- **How long or how far:** duration and distance.

| English | Greek |
| --- | --- |
| I see my friend. | Βλέπω **τον** φίλο μου. |
| I open the door. | Ανοίγω **την** πόρτα. |
| I'm buying a car. | Αγοράζω **ένα** αυτοκίνητο. |
| I'm waiting for an hour. | Περιμένω **μια** ώρα. |
| I work all day. | Δουλεύω όλη **τη** μέρα. |

**σε + article joins into one word:**

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Singular | στον | στη(ν) | στο |
| Plural | στους | στις | στα |

- Πάω **στον** κινηματογράφο. (I'm going to the cinema.)
- Βρέχομαι **στη** βροχή. (I get wet in the rain.)
- Καθόμαστε **στο** ίδιο τραπέζι. (We sit at the same table.)

### Genitive: "of" and possession

Use the genitive for possession, origin or relationship. Think of English "of" or "'s." In Greek, the genitive comes **after** the thing owned.

| English | Greek |
| --- | --- |
| The teacher's book | Το βιβλίο **του** δασκάλου |
| The door of the house | Η πόρτα **του** σπιτιού |
| The cat's name | Το όνομα **της** γάτας |
| The children's toys | Τα παιχνίδια **των** παιδιών |
| A friend's house | Το σπίτι **ενός** φίλου |
